Build guide · one board, one API key

Mikkey

A keychain-sized AI buddy who physically cannot speak. He only sings. That was a design decision and I regret it.

Mikkey hangs off your keys. His screen is his mouth, animated in sync with his own audio so he looks like he's forming the words. He has a microphone, so he hears you — ask him something in English, Russian, Kazakh or Mandarin and he sings the answer back in the language you used. He wears a small 3D-printed hat. It does nothing. It stays.

This guide takes you from nothing to a singing keychain in about half an hour. You need one off-the-shelf board and one API key. Everything else is in the repo.

2How it works

The stick is the body; your laptop is the brain. They talk over USB serial, or over TCP once the stick joins WiFi. The device is the authority over the link — it announces itself with POLL on whichever transport it is reading, and the laptop only pushes audio in reply.

[ M5StickS3 ] <---- USB serial OR TCP over WiFi ----> [ laptop ] screen / mic mikkey_server.py speaker / button :8090 http :8091 tcp hold the button --> mic audio --> faster-whisper --> OpenRouter (in character) | speaker + mouth <-- PCM + envelope <-- Fish Audio S2.1 <----------+
PieceWhat it does
Fish Audio S2.1 ProSings every line. You direct the delivery in square brackets, like a session singer: [breathy, sing-song, like a lullaby]. Same words, different character. This is why Mikkey exists — with a flat voice there is no Mikkey.
faster-whisperHis ears, running locally on the laptop. Auto-detects the language among an allowlist so short noisy clips don't wander off into Finnish.
OpenRouterHis brain. One short sung line per answer, in the language you spoke, with an English direction tag.
Mouth-sync envelopeThe laptop computes a loudness envelope alongside the audio and ships both. The face opens and closes on the envelope, so he looks like he's forming the words.
M5UnifiedThe Arduino library behind the screen, speaker, mic, IMU and button.

3Build it

1Python side
python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt

Pulls in the Fish Audio SDK, faster-whisper, numpy, pyserial and sounddevice. The first run downloads the whisper model (a few hundred MB), once.

2Keys
cp .env.example .env
# then edit .env:
FISH_API_KEY=sk-fish-your-key-here
OPENROUTER_API_KEY=sk-or-your-key-here   # optional, the brain

.env is gitignored. Keep it that way — never commit it.

3Arduino toolchain
brew install arduino-cli
arduino-cli core update-index --additional-urls \
  https://raw.githubusercontent.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/gh-pages/package_esp32_index.json
arduino-cli core install esp32:esp32 --additional-urls \
  https://raw.githubusercontent.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/gh-pages/package_esp32_index.json
arduino-cli lib install M5Unified

Known-good here: arduino-cli 1.4.1, esp32 core 3.3.8, M5Unified 0.2.20, M5GFX 0.2.27.

4Tell him where the laptop is

Plug the stick in over USB, then:

./set_wifi.sh "your-network" "your-password"

That writes firmware/mikkey/wifi_config.h (gitignored — it holds your WiFi password) with your laptop's mDNS name and current IP, then compiles and flashes in one go. USB alone is enough to get him singing; WiFi is what frees him from the cable.

5Flash by hand, if you'd rather
FQBN="esp32:esp32:esp32s3:CDCOnBoot=cdc,FlashSize=8M,PSRAM=opi,PartitionScheme=default_8MB"
arduino-cli compile --fqbn "$FQBN" firmware/mikkey
arduino-cli upload  --fqbn "$FQBN" -p /dev/cu.usbmodem* firmware/mikkey

Those flags matter: CDCOnBoot=cdc gives you the USB serial link, and PSRAM=opi is what lets him hold a whole clip in memory before playing it.

6Start the brain
./run.sh

Preflight checks, then the server with auto-restart and timestamped logs. It pre-generates every line in script.yaml at startup, so they play instantly later. Leave it running.

7Make him sing

In a second terminal:

.venv/bin/python mikkey_type.py

mikkey> Hello, I am Mikkey, and I live on your keys.

That's it. A blue dot in the top-left corner of his screen means the TCP link is up; grey means he's talking over USB instead. Both work.

4Living with him

Do thisHe does this
Tap the buttonSings the next line of script.yaml, in order.
Hold the buttonPush-to-talk. Speak, release, and he sings an answer back in the language you used.
mikkey_type.pyType a line, he sings it. /dir <words> sets the standing direction; a typed [tag] overrides it for one line. /ls, /l <n|id>, /next and /goto drive the script from the keyboard.
localhost:8090/studioThe same thing in a browser: two fields, direction and line. Change only the direction and sing again — that contrast is the whole demo.
localhost:8090/previewHis face on your screen, so you can see the animation without squinting at a 1.14″ LCD.
Look in out/Every line he has ever sung, as an audio master. out/takes.log records what played, when, and which file it is.

5Make him yours

To changeEdit
What he saysscript.yaml. Each line has an id and text; changing the text regenerates the audio on the next server start.
How he says itThe bracket tag at the front of any line. [sung like a stadium rock anthem, powerful, shouty] and [breathy, sing-song, like a lullaby] on the same words is the demo worth doing first.
Who he isThe SYSTEM prompt in mikkey_server.py — his character, the one-short-line rule, and the rule that he answers in the language he was spoken to.
His faceFACE_STYLE in firmware/mikkey/mikkey.ino: 2 = 1-bit pop-star line art, 1 = a full-screen grid face, 0 = landscape scenes. The line art is ASCII in tools/mj_art.py — run it to regenerate the header and the /mj preview. FACE_ROTATION picks which end is up, for your hat.
His voicemodel in script.yaml. Pin a specific voice with reference_id.
Languages he'll acceptMIKKEY_LANGS (default en,ru,kk,zh). An allowlist beats free auto-detection on short, noisy clips.

6Things that will otherwise cost you an hour

WiFi — the one that really bites

Home routers with band steering or a weak 2.4 GHz radio will simply refuse the stick. The reliable fallback is a phone hotspot with Maximize Compatibility turned on. The stick prints dbg wifi … on USB serial: reason 2 or 4 means the access point never answered, 15 means the WPA2 handshake timed out. USB works regardless, so a stubborn network never blocks you.

USB serial — do not regress this

Host writes must be paced at roughly 250 kB/s or the USB CDC link stalls permanently, and the receive ring silently drops bytes if you flood it while the firmware is drawing. The server already paces streams at about 1.4× real-time. If you rewrite the transport, keep the pacing.

Battery

250 mAh is under an hour of singing. For a long session, run him off a USB power bank.

Kazakh

Whisper detects Russian and Mandarin reliably but hears Kazakh as Turkish, at both base and small. Pin the language on /studio before a Kazakh exchange, then set it back to auto.

7When something is wrong

SymptomCause and fix
"mikkey_server.py is not running"Start ./run.sh first. The terminal and the browser tools are both clients of it.
Server won't startMissing FISH_API_KEY in .env, or no .venv. The preflight in run.sh names which.
Nothing on USBNo /dev/cu.usbmodem* — reseat the cable, and use a data cable, not a charge-only one.
He's silent but the screen worksLook at the server log. Generation failures print a reason; a failed line is requeued rather than skipped.
A line sounds wrongChange only the bracket tag and sing it again. Keep the words and punctuation identical — an added ellipsis rephrases the whole line.
Where's the audio?out/<id>.mp3 or .wav, indexed by out/takes.log.

Everything he can fail at, he fails out loud: a broken brain or a dead TTS call becomes a sung apology, a dropped link requeues the clip and replays it, and errors put a face and a reason on the screen. He is never silently broken.
